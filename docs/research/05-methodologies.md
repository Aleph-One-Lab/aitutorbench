# Methodologies for Evaluating AI Tutors: a taxonomy (research slice, 2026-10-05)

Scope: how people measure whether an AI tutor is good, independent of named benchmarks. Costs are rough per-evaluation estimates (the researcher's unless cited).

## 1. Expert pedagogical rubrics and human ratings

[LearnLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429) has raters role-play learners from scenarios, then pedagogy experts score transcripts on 29 rubric questions (7-point scale) covering active learning, metacognition, curiosity, adaptivity; LearnLM led on almost all questions, with preference strengths of +31% vs GPT-4o and +11% vs Claude 3.5 Sonnet. [Maurya et al. (NAACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.57/) define eight dimensions grounded in learning science (mistake identification, mistake location, revealing the answer, guidance, actionability, coherence, tone, human-likeness) and release MRBench: 192 conversations, 1,596 responses, seven LLM and human tutors with gold labels. The [BEA 2025 shared task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10579) built on this with 50+ teams; best macro-F1 was only 71.8 (mistake identification) and 58.3 (guidance), showing even reproducing expert labels is hard. Khan Academy combines classroom observation, interviews and transcript review ([blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)). Valid for pedagogical-move quality; failure modes: low agreement on subjective dimensions (tone, human-likeness), rubric overfitting once a lab trains on its own rubric, and no link to outcomes.

## 2. LLM-as-judge for pedagogy and its validity

The largest audit, [Reliability without Validity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544) (21 judges, ~541k judgments), finds exact-match agreement overstates discriminative ability by 33–41 pp vs Cohen's kappa, production judges with position bias >0.10 despite >0.95 test-retest reliability, and rankings shifting up to 14 places across benchmarks. For tutoring specifically, a [pre-registered audit of 1,179 tutor turns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28128) found LLM-judged "helpfulness" barely separated pedagogical from conversational policies (Cliff's δ 0.10 vs 1.0 under a pedagogy rubric) and its ordering reversed between judges; answer-revealing turns reliably reduced student independent work regardless of judge. Maurya et al. report Prometheus2 correlating poorly, sometimes negatively, with human pedagogy labels. [Human and LLM assessment of teaching acts](https://aclanthology.org/2025.codi-1.15.pdf) shows decomposing criteria and forcing reasoning per criterion raises agreement. [Augmenting human evaluation with LLM judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354) gives a doubly-robust two-stage design to decide how many human ratings to keep. Valid for decomposed rubric adherence after kappa-based validation; invalid for generic helpfulness or as the sole signal.

## 3. Simulated students

Lineage: SimStudent → generative students (Lu & Wang 2024, item-level correlation with real students) → EduAgent (2024) → [Classroom Simulacra (CHI 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02780v1) → [ParaStudent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12674) (fine-tuned on real code revisions; AUC 0.80 on feedback uptake vs near-chance for prompted simulators) → [StudentSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591) → [EduClaw-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03206), which grounds a learner in a knowledge-tracing model trained on real data, runs 30-day relationships over 55 scenarios, reports calibration (ECE 0.049) plus a classroom field study, and finds nearly all agent/model combinations fail to sustain quality. [SimulatorArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05444) (909 conversations, 450 math tutoring) finds profile-conditioned simulators reach Spearman 0.7 with human rankings of assistants. Evidence on realism is sobering: [Substance or Illusion? (ACL 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04025) finds prompted simulators perform poorly and fine-tuning helps but stays limited; [Towards Valid Student Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05473) names the "competence paradox" and proposes epistemic fidelity; [teachers tutoring LLM students (BEA 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bea-1.8.pdf) report they do not struggle realistically, forget inconsistently, and lack metacognition. No study yet shows that simulated learning gains predict real learning gains across tutors; treat as a cheap regression and stress test, not an outcome measure.

## 4. Learning-gain measurement

The criterion measure. Recent RCTs: World Bank [Nigeria](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee) (Copilot/GPT-4, six weeks, 0.31 SD composite, 0.23 SD English, among the most cost-effective programs); [Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6) (194 Harvard students, crossover, 0.73–1.3 SD vs active learning, but immediate post-test only, two topics, one institution; see [critique](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)); [Khanmigo two-year cluster RCT](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551) (18 schools, 1.3 percentile ranks per term, ~0.06–0.08 SD/year, indistinguishable from Khan practice without AI); [Eedi/LearnLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633) (165 students, +5.5 pp on novel problems). Standards: [WWC Handbook v5](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/referenceresources/Final_WWC-HandbookVer5_0-0-508.pdf) and [ESSA tiers](https://ies.ed.gov/rel-midwest/2025/01/aligning-evidence-based-clearinghouses-essa-tiers-evidence): Tier 1 needs an RCT meeting WWC standards without reservations, significant positive effect, ≥350 participants, multi-site; Tier 2 QED; Tier 3 correlational; Tier 4 rationale. Bloom's 2 sigma is not a benchmark: Cohen/Kulik/Kulik found 0.33; [VanLehn 2011](https://www.emergingedtech.com/-kvanlehn/Stringent/PDF/EffectivenessOfTutoring_Vanlehn.pdf) 0.79 human vs 0.76 ITS; [Kulik & Fletcher 2016](https://www.ida.org/-/media/feature/publications/w/we/welch-award-2017---effectiveness-of-intelligent-tutoring-systems-a-meta-analytic-review/1-effectivenessits.ashx) median 0.66 but 0.13 on standardized tests; [Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476) pooled 0.37 SD across RCTs ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/), [Nintil](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)). Failure modes: researcher-made vs standardized tests, short durations inflating novelty effects, immediate post-tests, attrition (30.7% in Medly), ceiling effects, Hawthorne effects.

## 5. Pairwise preference and arena-style evaluation

Google's [learning arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24477): 189 educators role-played learners across 49 scenarios in blind multi-turn conversations with model pairs (1,333 matchups); 206 independent experts gave pairwise preferences converted to Elo via Bradley-Terry; Gemini 2.5 Pro won 73.2% of non-tied matchups. A [blind text-only comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08702) found teaching-experienced annotators preferred LLM tutors over human tutors, 80% on empathy, a reminder that preference tracks fluency and warmth, not learning. Methodological fixes: [am-ELO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03475) models annotator reliability; [draw semantics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02306) matter for rankings. Valid for relative pedagogical preference among experts; failure modes: role-players are not learners, position and verbosity bias, matchup sampling, and a vendor running its own arena.

## 6. Conversation-level and longitudinal vs turn-level

Most rubrics (MRBench, BEA tracks) score a single next turn. Whole-dialogue evaluation changes conclusions: [SafeTutors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17373) shows pedagogical failures rising from 17.7% single-turn to 77.8% multi-turn; [EduClaw-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03206) shows almost all agents fail to sustain quality over 30 days; [Rethinking Scaffolding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766) (9,490 chats across nine benchmark and deployment datasets) shows real students bypass tutor scaffolding, so benchmark assumptions of high uptake are wrong. [MathTutorBench](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389392159_MathTutorBench_A_Benchmark_for_Measuring_Open-ended_Pedagogical_Capabilities_of_LLM_Tutors) and [SimulatorArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05444) (7.8-turn average) are intermediate. True longitudinal evidence (Khanmigo two years) is rare and expensive; turn-level is cheap but blind to drift, role collapse and repetition.

## 7. Engagement, retention, affect, motivation

The [Khanmigo trial](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551) is the key cautionary case: 96% tried it, but the median student messaged on one-third of practice days and in 17% of sessions with a mistake, mostly bare answers or suggested-prompt clicks; authors call engagement the binding constraint ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/08/25/ai-tutoring-students-khanmigo-khan-academy-engagement-study/)). Khan now scores cognitive engagement automatically on a passive-active-constructive (ICAP) scale ([blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)). Classic learning analytics: Baker's sensor-free detectors of boredom, confusion, frustration and gaming the system in Cognitive Tutors; gaming predicts poorer learning and boredom precedes it ([EDM 2012](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2012/uploads/procs/Full_Papers/edm2012_full_1.pdf), [LearnLab](https://learnlab.org/wiki/index.php?title=Gaming_the_system)). Self-reported engagement was higher in [Kestin et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6). Failure modes: perceived engagement and presence do not correlate with gains ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06078)); novelty decay; engagement optimized at the expense of struggle.

## 8. Safety, accuracy and hallucination audits for education

[Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute](https://institute.commonsensemedia.org/risk-assessments) rates products on eight principles (safety, effectiveness, fairness, dignity, real relationships, trustworthiness, data protection, transparency) via research plus hands-on testing, on a four-level scale; Khanmigo Low Risk, Gemini K-12 High Risk, [AI teacher assistants Moderate Risk](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds) (Aug 2025: bias, curriculum misalignment, unreviewed outputs). The UK DfE's [Generative AI product safety expectations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations) (Jan 2025; now 13 "standards", Jan 2026) are outcome-focused: filtering, monitoring, security, data protection, IP, transparency. Research-grade: [SafeTutors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17373) (11 harm dimensions, 48 sub-risks, answer over-disclosure and misconception reinforcement as harms) and [EduEVAL-DB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15531). EEF's contribution is trials rather than audits. Failure modes: snapshots of fast-changing products, vendor-selected configurations, no learning measure, and generic toxicity metrics missing pedagogy-specific harms.

## 9. Process measures from learning science

Mediators that can be scored automatically: [Tutor CoPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017) classified 550k+ messages and showed treated tutors asked more guiding questions and gave away fewer answers, alongside a 4 pp mastery gain (9 pp for lower-rated tutors); the [helpfulness audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28128) uses deterministic answer-leakage and student-independent-work detectors; [Chatbot Scaffolding and Student Uptake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766) score both sides per turn; [dialogue knowledge tracing (LAK 2025)](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/Dialogue_KT_LAK_25-2.pdf) infers mastery and misconceptions from tutoring talk; [scaffolding collapse](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19371v1) catalogues answer leakage, over-explanation, no-question collapse, misconception neglect and role drift. Valid as mechanisms, especially when paired with outcomes (as in CoPilot). Failure modes: Goodharting (a question every turn), scaffolding that students ignore, misconception diagnosis needing ground truth, and fading that cannot be judged from one session.

## 10. Cost, latency and accessibility

[Latency and cost of multi-agent tutoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24110) (four-agent Gemini 2.5 Flash system, 3,000+ live requests, up to 50 concurrent users) shows only the priority tier holds sub-4-second responses under classroom load, at roughly $4.79 vs $2.63 per student per semester. Khan treats latency as a primary metric and cut 3 s via agent optimization ([blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)). Tutor CoPilot cost $20 per tutor per year; the Nigeria study reports cost-effectiveness in learning per dollar. Accessibility has no standard tutor-specific methodology; DfE standards and reading-level, language and device constraints are the proxies used. Cheap to measure, but easily ignored in model-centric benchmarks.

## 11. Teacher-facing and classroom-level evaluation

EEF's Teacher Choices Trials randomize teachers: the [Aila trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/new-trial-ai-powered-lesson-planning) (~450 KS2 teachers, 86 schools, NFER-evaluated, results autumn 2026) measures planning time over a term plus lesson quality judged by a blind panel; the earlier [ChatGPT lesson-prep trial](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf) found 31% less planning time. Human-in-the-loop designs use teacher approval as a quality signal: Eedi tutors approved 76.4% of LearnLM drafts with minimal edits ([RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633)). Common Sense's teacher-assistant review covers Gemini for Classroom, Khanmigo Teacher, Curipod and MagicSchool. Valid for workload and teacher outcomes; weak link to student learning unless combined with Section 4.

## 12. Evaluating deployed products end to end

Credible product evaluations evaluate the system, not the model: [Khanmigo two-year cluster RCT](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551) (coach mode, daily remedial sessions, standardized percentile outcomes); [Nigeria Copilot](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee) (six weeks, 0.31 SD); [Medly practitioner-led micro-RCTs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14789) (929 students, g = 0.33 on curriculum-aligned tests, 30.7% attrition, explicitly preliminary); the pre-registered [Eedi scaled RCT](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/18079) (~1,200 students, 10 schools, four arms incl. human tutors, STAR Maths, April–June 2026); and Khan's continuous A/B testing over 15M+ threads using next-item correctness without AI help as the metric (+6.1% cumulative). Failure modes: engagement, not model quality, dominates effects; vendor-run studies; short windows; outcome measures aligned to the product.

## Which combinations are credible

- **Rubric + validated judge + outcomes**: Train an LLM judge on a learning-science rubric (Maurya/LearnLM style), validate with Cohen's kappa against ≥200 expert-labelled conversations ([Reliability without Validity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544)), then show the rubric score predicts a learning outcome in at least one RCT. Nobody has fully closed this loop yet; it is the open prize for AI Tutor Bench.
- **Process mediators + RCT** (Tutor CoPilot pattern): pair deterministic detectors (answer leakage, guiding questions, student uptake) with a randomized outcome, so the mechanism and the effect are shown together.
- **Arena for ranking, RCT for calibration**: use expert pairwise preference to order many models cheaply, then anchor the scale with a few real-student trials to learn how many Elo points equal a meaningful SD.
- **Multi-turn and longitudinal before single-turn**: any claim should survive SafeTutors-style multi-turn stress and an EduClaw-style multi-session run; single-turn leaderboards systematically overstate quality.
- **Simulated students as regression tests, never as outcomes**: use KT-grounded or fine-tuned simulators to catch collapse and leakage between releases, and report sim-to-real validation (calibration, field correlation) alongside.
- **Engagement as a gate on learning claims**: report usage distributions (not means) with every outcome; the Khanmigo trial shows a strong model with 17% uptake produces ~0 effect.
- **Safety and cost as pass/fail gates, not scores**: DfE standards and Common Sense principles decide eligibility; latency under classroom load and $/student decide deployability; neither should be averaged into a pedagogy score.
- **Pre-registration and WWC alignment for anything called "evidence"**: pre-register (as Eedi did), use standardized or externally built tests, ≥350 participants across sites, and report attrition and delayed post-tests; without these, Bloom-style effect sizes are marketing.
