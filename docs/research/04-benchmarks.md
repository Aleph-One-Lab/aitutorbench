## Benchmark notes and headline results

**TutorBench (Scale AI, 2025).** 1,490 expert-curated prompts across physics, chemistry, biology, calculus, statistics, CS; three use cases; >50% include images of student work. Judge is Claude-4-Sonnet with 0.78 human agreement. At launch no model exceeded ~56% (Gemini 2.5 Pro 55.7%); adaptive explanation was weakest (47.3% avg). The live leaderboard now lists 26 models; "Muse Spark" leads at 68.55, then GPT-5.4-pro 56.62, Gemini 2.5 Pro 55.65 ([leaderboard](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/tutorbench)). Data on HF (ScaleAI/TutorBench); paper CC BY-NC-ND. Critique: single-turn, rubric-judge only, no student outcome.

**MathTutorBench (ETH, EMNLP 2025 oral).** Reward model separates expert from novice teacher responses at 0.84. Original win rates vs human teachers on scaffolding: LearnLM-1.5-Pro 0.64, LLaMA-3.1-70B 0.63, GPT-4o 0.50, SocraticLM 0.39, Llemma "ScienceTutor" 0.37, Qwen2.5-Math-7B 0.06, demonstrating the solving-vs-teaching trade-off. Current leaderboard: Gemini-3.1-Pro leads most categories, Claude Sonnet 4.6 leads pedagogy following (0.85); all run with reasoning disabled. Licence inconsistency: paper says CC BY 4.0, repo says CC BY-SA 4.0.

**MRBench + BEA 2025 shared task (MBZUAI).** Eight dimensions (mistake identification, location, revealing answer, guidance, actionability, coherence, tone, human-likeness) annotated on responses from GPT-4, Gemini, Sonnet, Llama-3.1-8B/405B, Mistral, Phi-3 plus expert/novice humans, built on MathDial and Bridge. The shared task drew 50+ teams; best macro-F1 58.3 (guidance) to 71.8 (mistake identification); tutor identification 97.0 ([findings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10579)). Follow-ups: 0.5B Bradley-Terry reward model reaching 0.74 pairwise accuracy ([arXiv 2603.24375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24375)); AITutor-EvalKit demo (EACL 2026). BEA 2026 did **not** run a tutoring shared task.

**EduBench (2025).** Nine scenarios including error correction, personalized support, emotional support, grading and question generation, scored by LLM judges on 12 aspects with human validation. A small model trained on it matched DeepSeek-V3/Qwen-Max on the test split. Synthetic prompts; mostly single-turn.

**LearnLM (Google, 2024–25).** The "evaluation-driven" paper defines seven benchmarks and a 29-item pedagogy rubric (cognitive load, active learning, metacognition, motivation, etc.); LearnLM-Tutor was rated highest on nearly all items vs prompted Gemini. The Dec 2024 report shows expert pairwise preference for LearnLM of +31% vs GPT-4o, +11% vs Claude 3.5 Sonnet, +13% vs Gemini 1.5 Pro ([arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429)). Rubrics are published; data and raters are not, so it is not reproducible by third parties.

**StudentBench (Handshake, Sep 2026).** The first large human learning-gain benchmark: 2,383 adults, 27-question GRE pre/post around one hour of tutoring. Pooled AI tutoring was statistically equivalent to expert human tutors (−0.58 pp, 90% CI [−2.18, 1.03]); six individual models reached equivalence; AI beat control by +6.9 pp (Quant) / +5.5 pp (Verbal); Gemma 4 31B matched humans at 918× lower cost. Limitations stated by authors: no retention follow-up, adult English-speaking Handshake users only, no practice-alone control, and the "teaching quality" leaderboard is based on expert ratings rather than gains ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28470)).

**EduClaw-Bench (Aug 2026).** Only benchmark with a 30-day continuous tutor–learner relationship; the learner's answers are driven by a knowledge-tracing model trained on real student data, so learning gain is probed directly. Finding: almost no model/agent combination sustains effective teaching over the horizon. Calibration against a live classroom claimed; institution and code not verified.

**TeachBench, KMP-Bench, OpenLearnLM, MMTutorBench, ELBench, Elmes\*, OmniEduBench (Chinese/Korean groups, 2025–26).** TeachBench measures teaching by simulated-student score change on Gaokao items; example problems did not help. KMP-Bench covers six K-8 pedagogy principles plus skills, with a 150k-dialogue training pile. OpenLearnLM (124K items) finds no model dominates knowledge, skill and attitude; Grok-4.1 led knowledge but showed alignment concerns. MMTutorBench (685 image problems) finds OCR pipelines degrade tutoring and rubric LLM-judging is reliable. ELBench finds safety and teaching utility negatively correlated (r = −0.83) and education-specialised models underperforming. Elmes\* auto-generates rubrics for 330 scenarios; InnoSpark scored highest in human eval.

**SafeTutors (EMNLP 2026).** Pedagogical-safety taxonomy (11 harms, 48 sub-risks). Key number: pedagogical failures rise from 17.7% single-turn to 77.8% in extended dialogue, so single-turn benchmarks mask failure.

**Socratic benchmarks.** SocraticBench: best model (Gemma 3) only ~23% success with an expert-calibrated judge; size did not help; success plateaus near 8 rounds. SID (10k turns) and GuideEval both find frontier models fail at adaptive redirection when students are confused.

**TutorGym (AIED 2025).** Grounds tutoring in 223 ITS domains; LLMs were at chance labelling incorrect student steps and 52–70% on next-step actions, yet produced human-like learning curves as students.

**Student-simulation and sim-validity work.** SimulatorArena (909 human–LLM conversations incl. math tutoring) shows profile-conditioned simulators reach Spearman ρ≈0.7 with human ratings. ParaStudent (programming) and StudentSim (chess, L2 writing, math; beats GPT-5.4 on fidelity) train realistic students. Scarlatos et al. (ACL 2026) find prompted student simulators "perform poorly" and even fine-tuned ones remain limited, a direct threat to simulator-based tutor benchmarks. Apartsin et al. (May 2026) propose controllable skill-vector students. Ai2's TutorMoments adds 462 real K-12 sessions with 520 key moments continued by 7 tutor models (paper not located).

**Misconception diagnosis.** Eedi's 2024 Kaggle (map distractors to misconceptions, MAP@25, 1,446 teams) and 2025 MAP competition (classify misconceptions from free-text explanations, MAP@3, 1,850+ teams, ~40k submissions). Diagnostic only; no tutoring response is scored.

**Industry/vendor evaluations.** Khan Academy released 188 Khanmigo conversations (CoMTA) under a restrictive internal-evaluation licence, and an AIED 2026 paper on its metrics; a secondary source (respan.ai) describes an ICAP-based LLM judge scoring ~20% of conversations nightly, unverified. OpenAI's Study Mode (Jul 2025) shipped without a published evaluation; in Mar 2026 OpenAI announced a "Learning Outcomes Measurement Suite" with Stanford SCALE tracking persistence, motivation and problem-solving over time (Axios; openai.com page returned 403, no public data found). Vals SAGE grades handwritten undergraduate math work (Claude Opus 4.7 56.1%, Oct 2026), proprietary; it is grading, not tutoring. Common Sense Media rated AI *teacher assistants* "Moderate Risk" (Aug 2025) and gave Khanmigo 4 stars; there is no student-tutor pedagogy benchmark there. Stanford's Tutor CoPilot RCT (900 tutors, 1,800 students, +4 pp mastery, +9 pp for weaker tutors) is the key field evidence but not a benchmark.

**Datasets often cited as benchmarks but not scored:** MathDial, CIMA, Bridge, TutorChat, ConvoLearn (2,134 Earth-science dialogues, CC BY), Eedi Question-Anchored-Tutoring-Dialogues-2k. CLASS/SPOCK is a design framework. "ScienceTutor" appears to be the Llemma-7B-32K tutoring model from the TutorEval paper, not a benchmark.

**Not found / unverified.** No LMArena education category, HELM education subset, or Artificial Analysis education index was found before the search budget expired. "The Missing Evaluation Axis" (AIED 2026, 10k student submissions) sits behind Springer auth. The Pedagogy Benchmark leaderboard values were not fetched.

## Coverage gaps

1. **Durable learning and retention.** Only StudentBench measures real gain (immediate, one hour) and only EduClaw-Bench models a long horizon (simulated). Nothing measures retention after weeks, transfer, or spaced sessions with real learners.
2. **Real multi-turn sessions with real students.** Nearly all turn-level benchmarks score one response in a human-authored context. SafeTutors shows failure rates quadruple across turns; no public benchmark scores full live sessions at scale.
3. **Validated student simulators.** Simulator-based benchmarks rest on simulators that ACL 2026 work shows are unreliable; no shared standard for simulator fidelity gates their use.
4. **K-12 beyond math.** Writing feedback, reading comprehension, history, and language learning (L2-Bench aside) are thin; coding tutoring has only 17-item CSTutorBench and ParaStudent.
5. **Affect, motivation and engagement.** Rubrics include "tone" but none measure frustration handling, persistence, confidence or help-seeking behaviour with validated instruments.
6. **Misconception diagnosis → remediation loop.** Eedi scores diagnosis; MRBench scores remediation of a given error; nothing scores the full loop of eliciting, diagnosing, and verifying repair.
7. **Equity and accessibility.** No benchmark disaggregates tutor quality by learner language, SEND status (only the knowledge MCQs do), or reading level.
8. **Judge validity and contamination.** Most benchmarks use LLM judges validated on small samples; MathDial/GSM8k/Bridge are in training data; private sets (Scale, Vals) are opaque. No benchmark reports judge bias toward verbosity or politeness, which Abdulsalam & Aroyehun show inflates ratings.
9. **Product-level evaluation.** Benchmarks test raw models; Khanmigo, Study Mode, Learning Mode, LearnLM-in-Gemini are products with system prompts, tools and guardrails, and no independent, reproducible head-to-head exists.
10. **Cost/latency-adjusted pedagogy.** Only StudentBench reports cost per percentage point gained; nothing tracks it on a public leaderboard.
