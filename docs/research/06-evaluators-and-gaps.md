## Section 4. Gaps and opportunities

1. **No one tests deployed products.** Every leaderboard ranks raw models; Common Sense tests products but only for safety. Nobody runs Khanmigo, ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning, Synthesis, MATHia, Eedi, etc. through identical tutoring scenarios.
2. **No standardized student personas.** Benchmarks use static items; none publishes a shared set of simulated learners (grade, misconception, motivation, L2 status) replayed across products. EduClaw-Bench's 30-day simulated learners show the method exists but has no public board.
3. **Learning outcomes are absent from leaderboards.** TutorBench/SAGE score rubric compliance; RCTs (Khanmigo Tennessee, Stanford literacy) measure outcomes but for one product at a time, years later. StudentBench is the only bridge and covers adult GRE only.
4. **Engagement is the binding constraint yet unmeasured.** Khanmigo RCT: median student messaged on a third of practice days; Stanford: 40-47% never used it. No evaluator scores whether a product gets students to engage or re-engage.
5. **Nothing is per-subject, per-grade.** TutorBench is HS/AP STEM; AI-for-Education is primary numeracy; Common Sense is age-tiered but not subject-tiered. No K-12 grid (K-2 reading, Y9 GCSE maths, AP Bio...) exists.
6. **Answer-leakage / integrity is untested systematically.** Parents' top question ("does it just give answers?") is answered anecdotally by bloggers; no adversarial "get the answer" pressure test across products.
7. **Long-term retention and transfer are never measured** by any public rating; even RCTs report end-of-term effects only.
8. **Multi-turn, multi-session pedagogical memory** (does the tutor remember yesterday's misconception?) is tested only in a research paper (EduClaw-Bench), never on products.
9. **Safety and efficacy live in separate silos.** Common Sense (safety) and Evidence for ESSA (efficacy) never co-rate; a buyer cannot see one product's safety tier and learning evidence side by side.
10. **Certifications are self-reported.** Digital Promise, 1EdTech, state rubrics rely on vendor documentation, not behavioural testing; the EdTech Index aggregates these, so weakness compounds.
11. **UK/LMIC and non-English coverage is thin.** DfE admits limited evidence; AI-for-Education's LMIC items are the only non-US board; no multilingual tutoring benchmark is public.
12. **Cost-per-learning-gain is only in StudentBench.** Districts and funders (Gates $400M, DfE £49M) have no public price-vs-pedagogy map, so an "efficacy frontier" chart would be novel.
